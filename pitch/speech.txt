--- 3:35-4:55 | METHODOLOGY + RESULTS | slides 5-6 | [SPEAKER ?] ------------

[CLICK]

Why trust it? Because we gave machine learning a fair chance, and it lost.

With only eighty-five measured rates, a regression model on fifty-eight
features scored five percent error. That is statistically no better than
copying last month's test.

Our physics model: about two and a half percent. Twice as accurate, and the
gap is statistically solid.

Every score is time-forward: each prediction made only from the past.

And our favourite part: we never told the model any physics. It fitted
choke size to the power two point zero six, and pressure drop to the power
zero point four seven. The textbook says two, and one half. The model
rediscovered the choke-flow law from the data.

[CLICK]

Final scores: oil two point six percent error, water three point eight, gas
five point seven. R-squared above zero point nine nine on all three.

Researchers in Norway found the same thing: with only well tests, physics
beats machine learning.

And we're honest about the limits: on a well it has never seen, accuracy
collapses, so the app flags it instead of guessing.
