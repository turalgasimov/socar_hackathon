How much oil did well GL-04 produce yesterday?

Nobody knows. Not us, and not the engineer responsible for it.

In nine months, the ten wells in this field logged almost four million
sensor readings. But only eighty-five measured production rates. That is
about one test per well per month. Under API's well-testing practice,
MPMS 20.5, every rate between tests is allocated, not measured. And
research shows that error drops most going from one test a month to two.

[CLICK]

So between tests, engineers estimate, across separate tools and
spreadsheets. And what you can't see, you lose.

More than half of SOCAR's oil comes from gas-lifted wells, and every rate
you measure costs a well test of up to a day, or expensive hardware.

--- 3:30-5:20 | METHODOLOGY + RESULTS + WELL TESTS | slides 6-8 | [SPEAKER ?] -

[CLICK]

Why trust it? Because we gave machine learning a fair chance, and it lost.

With only eighty-five measured rates, a regression model on fifty-eight
features scored five percent error. That is statistically no better than
copying last month's test.

Our physics model: about two and a half percent. Twice as accurate, and the
gap is statistically solid.

Every score is time-forward: each prediction made only from the past.

And our favourite part: we never told the model any physics. It fitted
choke size to the power two point zero six, and pressure drop to the power
zero point four seven. The textbook says two, and one half. The model
rediscovered the choke-flow law from the data.

[CLICK]

Final scores: oil two point six percent error, water three point eight, gas
five point seven. R-squared above zero point nine nine on all three.

And we're honest about the limits: on a well it has never seen, accuracy
collapses, so the app flags it instead of guessing.

[CLICK]

Now the well tests. These are all six build-ups in the data, on the log-log
diagnostic. The dots are the measured pressure and its derivative; the lines
are our model of each well, fitted to every point. The fit error is under a
tenth of a psi, close to the gauge noise.

Permeability runs from 43 to 295 millidarcy. Three wells are stimulated,
three are damaged, and GL-01 and GL-10 reach a closed boundary. We also
tested the model on data it never saw: fitted on GL-01's first day, it
predicts the next two days at noise level.
